    What gets saved: GPS latitude and longitude plus the selected date in JPEG EXIF metadata. The printed map card is optional and contains an OpenStreetMap inset with attribution. Output is re-encoded as JPEG; other camera metadata may not be preserved. Exact GPS metadata can reveal someone's location; share carefully.

    How this works

    Choose a saved photo or capture one with your device camera. Camera access starts only after you press the camera button and stops after capture or when closed. Provide coordinates with browser location, map selection, place search or manual entry. Photos stay in this browser. Opening the map loads its visible tiles from OpenStreetMap. Creating a printed map card requests four nearby tiles and reveals the approximate area. A place search sends the typed query only when you press Search. Use current location looks up a nearby place label from GPS coordinates; Find place name looks up coordinates you entered. These actions send exact coordinates to OpenStreetMap when pressed. “Open this point in Google Maps” sends the coordinates to Google Maps in a new tab.

    This tool embeds coordinates as metadata. It does not verify that a photo was actually taken at that location. The location stamp can be turned off while GPS metadata remains in the JPEG.
